How Insurance Claims Work After Hurricane Damage

Published February 15, 2026 by American Disaster Solutions

Navigating the insurance claims process after hurricane damage can feel like a second disaster. Between confusing policy language, adjuster inspections, documentation requirements, and unfamiliar acronyms like TPA, most property owners feel overwhelmed before they even begin. This guide demystifies the entire process — from the moment you file your claim to the day you receive your settlement — so you know exactly what to expect and how to protect your interests.

What Is the First Step in Filing a Hurricane Damage Claim?

The first step is to contact your insurance company and file a notice of loss as soon as it is safe to do so after the storm. Most insurance policies require that you report damage within a specific timeframe — typically 24 to 72 hours for catastrophe events, though some policies allow up to 30 days for the initial notice. The sooner you file, the sooner an adjuster can be assigned and the claims process can begin.

When you call to report your claim, have the following information ready: your policy number, the date the damage occurred, a general description of the damage, your current contact information (especially if you have been displaced), and whether you have already taken any mitigation steps such as emergency tarping. The claims representative will assign you a claim number — write this down and keep it in a safe place, as you will need it for every future communication about your claim.

Many carriers now offer online and mobile app claim filing options, which can be faster than phone calls during major catastrophe events when call centers are overwhelmed. Regardless of how you file, request written confirmation of your claim submission and the name of the representative who took your report. This documentation establishes a clear timeline that protects you if any disputes arise later about when you reported the damage.

What Does an Insurance Adjuster Look for After a Hurricane?

After you file your claim, your insurance company will assign an adjuster to inspect the damage. The adjuster's job is to assess the scope and cause of damage, determine whether it is covered under your policy, and prepare a repair estimate. During a major hurricane event, it may take several days to several weeks for an adjuster to reach your property due to the sheer volume of claims filed simultaneously.

The adjuster will inspect the exterior of your property first, looking at the roof, siding, windows, gutters, fencing, and any outbuildings. On the roof, they will look for missing or damaged shingles, exposed decking, damaged flashing around vents and chimneys, and evidence of water intrusion at penetration points. They will also inspect the interior, checking ceilings, walls, floors, and contents for water damage, mold, and structural issues.

It is important that you or your representative be present during the adjuster's inspection. Walk through the property with them and point out all areas of damage, including anything they may not immediately see. Show them your documentation — photos, videos, and written notes from immediately after the storm. If you have had emergency tarping done, provide the tarping company's documentation showing what damage existed before the tarp was installed. This before-and-after documentation is invaluable because the adjuster cannot see the full extent of roof damage once a tarp is in place.

What Documentation Do You Need for a Hurricane Damage Claim?

Thorough documentation is the foundation of a successful insurance claim. At minimum, you need: pre-storm photos of your property (if available), post-storm photos and video of all damage areas, a written inventory of damaged personal property with estimated values, receipts for any emergency mitigation work (tarping, board-up, water extraction), receipts for temporary living expenses if you were displaced, and copies of all communication with your insurance company.

Your emergency tarping company should provide a complete documentation package including pre-tarp damage photos, a written scope of work describing the tarping method and materials used, measurements of both the damaged area and the tarped area, and post-installation photos. This package serves double duty: it supports your insurance claim and it proves you fulfilled your duty to mitigate further damage.

Keep a claim diary — a running log of every phone call, email, and interaction related to your claim. Record the date, time, name of the person you spoke with, and a summary of what was discussed. This may seem excessive, but during a major catastrophe event, claims can take months to resolve, and having a detailed record of every interaction protects you if there are disagreements about what was communicated or promised.

How Long Does a Hurricane Insurance Claim Take?

The timeline for a hurricane insurance claim varies widely depending on the severity of the event, the complexity of your damage, your carrier's claims volume, and your state's regulatory requirements. In general, you can expect the initial adjuster inspection within one to four weeks of filing, depending on the scale of the catastrophe. After the inspection, the adjuster typically submits their estimate within five to ten business days.

Once the estimate is approved, your carrier will issue an initial payment — usually within 30 days of the inspection in most states. This initial payment may not cover the full scope of repairs, especially if there is hidden damage that was not visible during the initial inspection. As repairs progress and additional damage is discovered, your contractor can submit a supplement request to the carrier for additional funds.

From initial filing to final settlement, a straightforward hurricane claim typically takes 60 to 120 days. Complex claims involving significant structural damage, contents losses, or disputed coverage can take six months or longer. If your claim is progressing too slowly, most states have prompt payment laws that require carriers to act within specific timeframes. Contact your state's department of insurance if you believe your carrier is unreasonably delaying your claim.

What Role Does Emergency Tarping Play in Your Claim?

Emergency tarping plays a critical role in the insurance claims process for two reasons. First, it fulfills your duty to mitigate — the contractual obligation in your policy to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage. Failing to tarp a damaged roof can give your carrier grounds to deny coverage for any secondary water damage that occurs after the initial storm event. Second, the tarping documentation provides essential evidence of the roof damage for the adjuster's review.

Emergency tarping is typically billed as a separate line item from the permanent repair estimate. Most carriers cover emergency tarping costs in full, separate from your deductible, because it falls under the emergency mitigation provisions of your policy. What Is a TPA and How Do They Affect Your Claim?

A TPA — Third-Party Administrator — is a company that insurance carriers hire to manage certain aspects of the claims process on their behalf. During major catastrophe events, carriers often engage TPAs to handle the surge in claims volume. The TPA may manage adjuster assignments, process documentation, authorize emergency mitigation work, and coordinate vendor deployments.

For property owners, working with a TPA adds another layer to the claims process. Instead of dealing directly with your carrier, you may find yourself coordinating with TPA representatives for certain aspects of your claim. The key thing to understand is that the TPA acts on behalf of your carrier — their decisions carry the same weight as decisions made by the carrier directly.
